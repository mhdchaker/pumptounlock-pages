PumpToUnlock works without an account, and we never ask for your name or email.

The camera picture that counts your push-ups is processed on your iPhone and is never uploaded. The app does send some data to services we use, stored under a random ID: how you use the app, your push-up numbers and your purchases. This page lists all of it. To see whether our ads work, the app also tells Meta and TikTok when it is installed and opened. Section 4 says what that includes and how to stop it.

1. Who is responsible

PumpToUnlock is made and run by El Mahdi Chaker, the seller named on the App Store. For any privacy question or request, write to pumptounlock@gmail.com.

2. In short

  • You need no account. We do not ask for your name, email address or phone number.
  • The app shows no ads. We do not sell your data and we do not give it to data brokers.
  • We advertise PumpToUnlock on Facebook, Instagram and TikTok. To measure those ads, the app tells Meta and TikTok when it is installed and opened. Your iPhone's advertising ID, which lets them match your use of the app to an ad you saw, is sent only if you allow it when your iPhone asks. In Europe, nothing is sent to Meta or TikTok before you allow it.
  • Pictures from the camera never leave your iPhone.
  • We cannot see which apps you block or how long you use them.
  • The app sends usage data, push-up numbers and purchase records to the service providers named in section 4, under a random ID.

3. Your random ID

When you first open the app, it gets random IDs for this installation: one for our database and analytics, and one from our purchase provider. An ID looks like 3f2a9c1e-7b4d and says nothing about who you are. Our purchase provider also receives Apple's vendor identifier for your iPhone. That is an ID Apple gives to the apps of one developer on a device. It is not the advertising ID. Meta's and TikTok's software in the app each create one more random ID for this installation. If you allow tracking (section 4), our purchase provider also receives your iPhone's advertising ID.

Everything in section 4 is stored under these IDs, never under your name. If you delete the app and install it again, you get new random IDs.

4. What the app sends, and who receives it

Usage analytics: Mixpanel

Mixpanel, Inc. in the United States runs our analytics. The app sends:

  • Which screens you open and how long you stay on them, and when you open and close the app.
  • What you do in the app, such as the onboarding steps you finish, the push-up sessions you start and end, changes to your blocked apps, the minutes you spend, the badges you earn or share, the reminders you turn on, what you tap on the paywall, and whether you allow the camera, Screen Time access and tracking when your iPhone asks.
  • The numbers that belong to these actions: push-ups counted, minutes earned, session length, your intensity setting, your minute balance, and how many apps and categories you block. Never which apps.
  • Purchases: the plan you chose, its price and currency, and later changes such as a renewal or a cancellation.
  • Your iPhone model, iOS version, app version, screen size, whether you are on Wi-Fi, and the language the app is set to.
  • Your approximate location: city, region and country. Mixpanel works this out from the IP address of your connection and does not store the IP address itself.

We use this to see whether the app works, where people get stuck and which features are used. Mixpanel deletes events after two years. A profile with running totals under your random ID, for example push-ups done, stays until we delete it.

Push-up sessions and settings: Supabase

Our database runs at Supabase, on servers in Ireland. The app sends:

  • After each push-up session: the number of push-ups, the minutes earned and the time of the session.
  • Your intensity setting and your minute balance.

Supabase's sign-in service also records the IP address your iPhone connected from.

We use this to keep a copy of your settings and to see overall numbers, such as how many push-ups are done with the app. This data stays until we delete it. There is no automatic deletion.

Trials and purchases: RevenueCat

RevenueCat, Inc. in the United States manages trials and purchases for us. The app sends:

  • For every installation: a random customer ID, Apple's vendor identifier, your iPhone model, iOS version, app version, language and App Store country.
  • When a paywall is shown and when it is closed.
  • If you start a trial or buy Elite: the product, price, currency and dates, and later renewals and cancellations, as confirmed by Apple.

We use this to unlock Elite on your iPhone, to restore purchases and to see how our plans perform. Payment itself runs through Apple. We never see your card or your Apple Account. RevenueCat keeps this data for as long as we use the service, or until you ask us to delete it.

Measuring our ads: Meta

We advertise PumpToUnlock on Facebook and Instagram. To see which ads bring people to the app, the app contains software from Meta: Meta Platforms Ireland Limited for people in Europe, Meta Platforms, Inc. in the United States for everyone else.

At the end of the setup, before the plans are shown, your iPhone asks whether PumpToUnlock may link your activity with data from other companies. Apple calls this tracking. What the app sends depends on your answer.

  • Until you have answered: the app tells Meta that it was installed, each time it is opened, and how long your previous visit lasted. On an iPhone set to a European region or time zone (the European Union, the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland), the app sends nothing to Meta until you have allowed it.
  • If you allow it: the same, and with it your iPhone's advertising ID. Our purchase provider RevenueCat then also receives your advertising ID, your IP address and Meta's random ID for this installation. RevenueCat tells Meta when you start a trial or buy Elite, with the product, price and currency.
  • If you say no: from then on the app sends nothing to Meta, and RevenueCat reports nothing about you to Meta.

With each report, Meta's software sends: its random ID for this installation, the app's ID and version, your iPhone model, iOS version, language and region setting, time zone, the name of your mobile carrier where iOS provides it, screen size, the number of processor cores, and whether you allowed tracking. Meta also sees the IP address of your connection.

The app never sends Meta your push-up numbers, your Screen Time data, the apps you block, your setup answers or anything from the camera. We have switched off the parts of Meta's software that would send screen titles, or samples for Meta's own product development.

If you set up PumpToUnlock before this question existed, you are not asked, and the app sends nothing about you to Meta.

Before your answer, the app reports to Meta only while you are in the setup. If you close the app after choosing your apps and before your iPhone has asked, the app opens without the setup the next time. You are then not asked and nothing is sent to Meta, unless you remove all your blocked apps and the setup starts again.

You can change your answer at any time: in your iPhone's Settings, under Privacy & Security, open Tracking and switch PumpToUnlock off. From the next time you open the app, nothing more is sent to Meta.

Meta keeps what it receives under its own IDs and uses it under its own policy. We cannot look this data up or delete it for you. More information: Meta Privacy Policy.

Measuring our ads: TikTok

We also advertise PumpToUnlock on TikTok, and the app contains software from TikTok for the same purpose. The one question your iPhone asks covers Meta and TikTok together, and the same rules apply.

  • Until you have answered: the app tells TikTok that it was installed, each time it is opened, and whether it was opened again the day after. On an iPhone set to a European region or time zone, the app sends nothing to TikTok until you have allowed it.
  • If you allow it: the same, and with it your iPhone's advertising ID. The app then also tells TikTok when you start a trial or buy Elite, with the product, and for a purchase the price and currency. Later renewals are not reported.
  • If you say no: from then on the app sends nothing to TikTok.

With each report, TikTok's software sends: its random ID for this installation, Apple's vendor identifier for your iPhone, the app's name, ID and version, your iPhone model, iOS version, language and region setting, and whether you allowed tracking. TikTok also sees the IP address of your connection.

The app never sends TikTok your push-up numbers, your Screen Time data, the apps you block, your setup answers or anything from the camera. If you set up PumpToUnlock before this question existed, you are not asked, and the app sends nothing about you to TikTok.

Before your answer, the app reports to TikTok only while you are in the setup. If you close the app after choosing your apps and before your iPhone has asked, the app opens without the setup the next time. You are then not asked and nothing is sent to TikTok, unless you remove all your blocked apps and the setup starts again.

You change your answer in the same place as for Meta: in your iPhone's Settings, under Privacy & Security, open Tracking and switch PumpToUnlock off.

TikTok keeps what it receives under its own IDs and uses it under its own policy. We cannot look this data up or delete it for you. More information: TikTok Privacy Policy.

Diagnostics of the push-up counter: Google ML Kit

The software that finds your posture in the camera picture is Google's ML Kit. It runs on your iPhone. According to Google, it sends technical data to Google LLC in the United States for diagnostics and usage analytics: device model, iOS version, the app's ID and version, an ID for the installation, performance figures such as speed, settings such as picture format and size, which functions ran, and error codes. Pictures are not on that list. We do not receive this data.

More information: ML Kit data disclosure for iOS and Google Privacy Policy.

Apple

Purchases run through the App Store, and Apple's privacy policy applies to them. The app also asks Apple's public App Store service which version is current, so it can tell you about an update. That request carries the app's ID and a country code, and nothing about you.

When you write to us

If you send us an email, we receive your email address and what you write. We use it only to answer you.

5. What stays on your iPhone

  • The live camera picture. It is analysed on your iPhone to count push-ups. No video is recorded, and nothing from the camera is uploaded.
  • One still picture for each badge. The first time you reach a badge, the app keeps one still picture from the camera for your badge picture. It stays in the app's storage on your iPhone, and in your own iPhone backup if you use one. It leaves your iPhone only when you share it yourself.
  • A photo you pick for a badge picture. It stays on your iPhone until you share it.
  • The apps you block and your Screen Time data. Apple's Screen Time system handles your selection through protected tokens, and the screen time figures in Insights are worked out on your iPhone. We cannot see which apps you block or use, or for how long.
  • Your onboarding answers and your reminders. Your goals and your estimate of daily phone time stay on your iPhone. Reminders are planned on your iPhone. We send no push messages from a server.

Deleting the app removes all of this.

6. Legal grounds and transfers

If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom, these are the legal grounds under the GDPR:

  • Keeping your settings and handling trials and purchases is needed to provide the app (Article 6(1)(b)).
  • Analytics and diagnostics rest on our legitimate interest in knowing whether the app works and how it is used (Article 6(1)(f)). You can object at any time by writing to us.
  • Sending your advertising ID, and reporting trials and purchases to Meta and TikTok, happens only with your consent (Article 6(1)(a)). You give it by choosing Allow, and you can withdraw it at any time in your iPhone's Settings.

Mixpanel, RevenueCat, Google and Meta process data in the United States. TikTok processes data in the countries named in its policy, which include countries outside Europe. These transfers rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or on the EU's standard contractual clauses.

The app has no switch to turn analytics off. What goes to Meta and TikTok is the exception: you stop it in your iPhone's Settings, as described in section 4. Deleting the app stops all collection.

7. Your rights, and how to delete your data

You have the right to see the data we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to restrict or object to its use, and to receive a copy of it. You can also complain to your data protection authority.

Write to pumptounlock@gmail.com. Because your data is stored under a random ID and not under your name, we need a way to find it:

  • If you started a trial or bought Elite, send us the order number from Apple's receipt email. We find your record with it and delete your purchase record, your analytics data and your database entries within 30 days. At our analytics provider the deletion can take a few weeks longer to complete. Deleting the record does not cancel a subscription. You cancel that in your App Store settings.
  • If you never started a trial or bought anything, nothing in our records tells us which ones are yours, so we cannot find them on request. They are not connected to your name, your email or your Apple Account.

Deleting the app removes everything stored on your iPhone and stops all further collection.

8. Children

We do not ask for anyone's age, and the app handles data the same way for every user. If you are a parent or guardian and have a question about your child's data, write to us.
